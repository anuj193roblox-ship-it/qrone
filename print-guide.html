Rule of thumb: the code's width is about a tenth of the scanning distance. Bigger is always safer, and long addresses need more room.

Five rules for printing

  1. Rule 1

    Never go below 2 cm (0.8 in)

    Each square in the pattern gets tiny on a small code. Older and cheaper phone cameras can't focus that close, so a code on a business card often fails exactly when someone tries it.

  2. Rule 2

    Keep the white border

    Phones find a code by its empty edge, called the quiet zone. Don't crop it, and don't print text or pictures right up to the code.

  3. Rule 3

    Dark ink on light paper

    White-on-black codes confuse some phone cameras, and pale colours like yellow on white fade into the paper under dim restaurant lights. Black or a deep navy on white is safest. Our maker warns you about weak colour pairs.

  4. Rule 4

    Watch out for glossy lamination

    Overhead lights bounce off gloss and wash out part of the code, so diners have to tilt the menu to scan it. Ask your printer for a matte finish, or keep the code away from the shiniest spot.

  5. Rule 5

    SVG for print shops, PNG for Word or Canva

    An SVG is drawn from shapes, so it stays sharp at any size, from a sticker to a billboard. A PNG is made of pixels: perfect for documents and social posts, but it blurs if it's stretched far beyond its size.

Always scan a printed test copy with two different phones before printing the whole run.
