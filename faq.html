How it works.

Do QR codes expire?

A static code never expires. It is your address drawn as a pattern. A dynamic code can stop working, because a company's server forwards each scan, and that company can turn the forwarding off.

Static or dynamic: what's the difference?

Static stores your real address in the pattern. Dynamic stores a short link owned by the QR service, which redirects to your page. Dynamic lets you change the destination later, but you depend on the service staying active.

How do I tell which one I have?

Use the checker. It shows whether a code points straight at a website or at a known QR-vendor short link.

Why would a dynamic code stop working?

Usually a trial or plan ending, a vendor changing its plans, or a vendor closing. Several vendors say on their own help pages that codes are deactivated after a trial. See how it works and which short links do this. The checker links to that page for each vendor it recognises.

Can I change where a static code points?

No. The address is part of the pattern, so a new destination needs a new code.

Does the checker upload my image?

No. It is read on your device. See privacy.

What does "unknown link" mean?

The code goes through a link shortener that isn't on our list. We can't say who runs it or what happens when an account ends.

Why do free editable codes stop after 14 days?

Free editable codes are for testing before you print. The 14-day limit stops free codes being used for spam or phishing, and keeps the free plan a trial rather than a permanent home. Static codes from the home page are free and never expire, and paid editable codes never expire either.

How can you charge only once?

Our hosting costs a fixed amount, about $5 a month, however many codes we serve. A handful of sales covers a year, so there's no need for a monthly fee. We don't sell your data or show ads to make up the difference. Read our promise.

What happens if QrOne shuts down?

You get at least 12 months' notice, and the redirect server's code will be published so anyone can run it (our promise). You can download every code and where it goes at any time, on every plan. If your codes use a domain you control, your web developer can load that file and the codes keep working without us. Codes on QrOne's own short-link address depend on QrOne, which is why using your own domain is the safest choice for anything printed in bulk.

What happens if I ask for a refund?

Ask within 14 days of paying for a full refund. Your account goes back to the plan you had before, and if that's Free, your codes keep working for 14 more days so nothing you printed breaks overnight. Refund policy.

Do you track how many times my code is scanned?

No. We don't count or store scans, and we set no cookies for the people who scan your codes. Cloudflare, which runs our servers, keeps technical request logs for up to 7 days so problems can be fixed.

How does the checker decide?

It compares the code's domain with a list of known QR-vendor short links. A vendor is only named when its own help or pricing page shows what happens to codes, and the checker links to that page. If a domain isn't on the list, it says so and doesn't guess. See the list.
